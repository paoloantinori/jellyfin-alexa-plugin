# French L1 to English L2 Perceptual/Production Assimilation Map

Generated: 2026-07-25. Purpose: ASR phonetic-synonym rule generation.

## Theoretical Framework

- Speech Learning Model (SLM), Flege 1995: similar L2 sounds assimilate to L1 categories via equivalence classification, blocking new category formation.
- PAM/PAM-L2, Best 1995; Tyler, Best, Faber and Levitt 2014.
- Cue weighting, Gottfried and Beddor 1988; Escudero and Boersma 2003: French speakers pay almost no attention to temporal (duration) information when differentiating vowels. Critical for English /i/-/ɪ/ and /ɛ/-/æ/ where duration is a primary cue.

## French Phoneme Inventory (canonical, Wikipedia French phonology)

### Consonants
- /p, b, t, d, k, ɡ/: dental laminal [t̪,d̪,n̪], /p,t,k/ unaspirated
- /f, v, s, z, ʃ, ʒ/: French has /ʒ/ natively (jour)
- /m, n, ɲ/: has /ɲ/ (gn)
- /l/: always clear, never velarized
- /ʁ/: uvular (fricative or approximant), not alveolar
- /j, ɥ, w/: glides
- NOT in French: /h/, /θ/, /ð/. /ŋ/ only in loanwords (camping), not native.

### Vowels (Parisian)
- Oral: /i, e, ɛ, a, y, ø, œ, ə, u, o, ɔ/
- Nasal: /ɑ̃, ɔ̃, ɛ̃/
- NO tense-lax contrast (no /ɪ/ or /ʊ/), no /æ/, no /ʌ/, no /ɜː/, no /ɑː/ or /ɔː/
- Diphthongs are phonetic sequences (glide+vowel), not phonemic. English-type diphthongs have no direct equivalent.

### Vowel key findings
1. /iː/ and /ɪ/ merge to /i/ (duration ignored)
2. /uː/ and /ʊ/ merge to /u/
3. /ɛ/ and /æ/ merge to /ɛ/ (most documented French-specific confusion)
4. /ʌ/ has no equivalent, maps to /a/ or /ɔ/
5. All diphthongs monophthongize or become hiatus (two syllables)
6. Vowel length distinctions lost

## 4. Actionable rules for PhoneticSynonymGenerator (French)

### Vowel
- /iː/ equals /ɪ/ (beat equals bit)
- /uː/ equals /ʊ/ (food equals foot)
- /ɛ/ equals /æ/ (bed equals bad)
- /ʌ/ to /a/ (cut becomes kat)
- /ʌ/ to /ɔ/ (cut becomes kɔt)
- diphthongs monophthongize (/aɪ/ to /ɛ/ or /ai/, /eɪ/ to /e/, /aʊ/ to /o/, /ɔɪ/ to /o/, /əʊ/ to /o/)

### Consonant
- /h/ delete (hat becomes at)
- /θ/ to /s/ (think becomes sink) - NOTE European French, distinct from Italian /θ/ to /t/
- /ð/ to /z/ (the becomes zə)
- /r/ to uvular (orthographic r unchanged but realization differs)
- /ŋ/ to /n/ or /ɲ/
- /tʃ/ to /ʃ/, /dʒ/ to /ʒ/

## 5. Cross-language note

French and German both map /θ/ to /s/ (not /t/), differing from Italian/Spanish which map /θ/ to /t/. This confirms per-language generators are necessary, a shared Romance rule would be wrong here. Quebec French (fr-CA) differs from European French: it uses /t/ and /d/ for the dental fricatives, matching the Italian pattern. The plugin has separate fr-FR and fr-CA locales, so this divergence should be respected if the generators are locale-specific.
